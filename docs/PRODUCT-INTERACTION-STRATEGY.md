# Charlie Architect — Product-led digital experiences

## Make the product experienced, not merely displayed
The most powerful differentiator may be one meaningful, product-specific interaction rather than ten visual effects. Design from what the visitor can *do* or *learn* with this particular object.

### Discovery sequence
1. Inspect any supplied image and distinguish visual facts, interpretation and unknown claims.
2. Define audience, emotional positioning, actual primary visitor task and business goal.
3. List three possible **signature interactions**, rank by product relevance, feasibility, honesty and accessibility, then choose the strongest.
4. Connect the hero, story sections and CTA to that interaction. Do not create interchangeable content sections.
5. Map limitations: genuine product assets vs illustrated views; usable real data vs a clearly labelled conceptual demo.

### Product-dependent examples
- **Keyboard:** an illustrated keyboard can respond to actual key presses and lead into an honest local typing exercise.
- **Camera:** demonstrate framing or image comparison using authorized images, without inventing optical specifications.
- **Perfume:** tell a visual material story; do not fake sensory claims or pretend the user can smell it.
- **Lighting:** interactive lighting scenes are useful only if presented as simulations or based on real product evidence.
- **Business dashboard:** a real editable workflow beats ornamental 3D.

These are examples, never templates to force on a different product.

## Creative art direction
- Let geometry, material, lighting, spacing, imagery and type be distinctive to the reference.
- Create a hero with an editorial composition and an immediately understandable action, not a default central heading and three cards.
- Use carefully staged, functionally justified scroll choreography. Separate intro spectacle from daily UI.
- Introduce motion only when the product narrative or interaction benefits from it.
- A product drawn in CSS/SVG from one low-resolution photo is **illustrative**, not an authenticated render of unknown details.

## Accessibility and privacy for interactive product visualizations
A decorative product model may use `aria-hidden`; a working keyboard, dial, gallery or other control may **not** be both operable and inaccessible. Provide semantic keyboard and touch equivalents.

If receiving physical-keyboard input, restrict listening to intended interactive contexts. Never log, store or transmit sensitive typing silently. Explain any local storage and its purpose accurately.

## Acceptance
A visitor should quickly be able to answer: What is this? What makes it different? What can I do? Does that interaction work? Why is the movement there?
